# Underdetermination and statistical modelling in diachronic corpus linguistics: a reanalysis of Walkden et al. (2023)
## Roland Schäfer (Friedrich-Schiller-Universität Jena)

## Draft

I reanalyse the corpus data of Walkden et al. (2023), which uses three historical corpus studies to assess Trudgill's theory from sociolinguistic typology (Trudgill 2011), according to which short-term adult-driven language contact leads to simplification effects in language. I argue, on methodological and epistemological grounds, that Studies 1 and 2 provide no evidence for the hypotheses they are taken to support. The problem is twofold. At the level of inference, the paper reads low p-values from Null-Hypothesis Significance Testing as corroboration of a large, loosely specified network of hypotheses, when such a test has almost no capacity to expose those hypotheses as false if they were indeed false. At the level of the data, both studies mishandle aggregation: Study 1 collapses thousands of binary outcomes into a few dozen equally weighted per-text difference scores, and Study 2 uses an analogous aggregate as a regressor. Refitting without these errors, I find that little survives. In Study 1, once texts enter as a random effect, between-text variation swamps dialect, and the one generalisation that holds rests essentially on a single text. In Study 2, the reported model is all but indistinguishable from a model with only a random effect for individual texts and no other explanatory variables at all. I close with three suggested remedies: (i) disciplined, severity-aware inference paired with more numerically committed theories, (ii) open data as a routine part of peer review, and (iii) support for publishing negative findings. More broadly, however, historical-corpus data of this kind might rarely support a confirmatory framework, and the deeper error is forcing exploratory-grade data through a confirmatory machinery they were never meant to pass through.

See: Walkden, George, Gemma Hunter McCarley, Raquel Montero, Molly Rolf, Sarah Einhaus, and Henri Kauhanen. 2023. “Sociolinguistic Typology Meets Historical Corpus Linguistics.” Transactions of the Philological Society 121 (3): 546–67. https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-968X.12275.
